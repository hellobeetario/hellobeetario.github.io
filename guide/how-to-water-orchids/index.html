How to water orchids and get the moth orchid to flower again

By Silvan, Beetario · updated 24 November 2026 · 5 min read

How to water orchids in short: about once a week in the growing season, a little less in winter, and always let the water drain away completely. The roots should never dry out fully and never stand in water. The moth orchid (Phalaenopsis) also wants a bright spot without direct summer sun and warmth above 16 °C. After flowering, a month in a cooler spot helps it flower again.

Which orchid do you have?

Most orchids on windowsills are moth orchids, botanically Phalaenopsis. You recognise them by their broad, leathery leaves, the grey aerial roots and the flat flowers on a long stem.

All information in this guide comes from the Royal Horticultural Society (RHS) growing guide for Phalaenopsis. Other orchids such as Cymbidium or Dendrobium partly need different care. If you are not sure which one you have, you can identify it with Beetario from a photo.

How to water orchids correctly

Knowing how to water orchids avoids most mistakes. The RHS recommends watering orchids lightly about once a week during the growing season and slightly less in winter. Two rules always apply: never let the roots dry out completely, and never let the plant sit in water.

When you water orchids, use tepid water, preferably rainwater. Make sure no water runs onto the leaves or into the centre of the plant, the crown. In hot weather you can mist the leaves and aerial roots daily or stand the pot on a wide saucer of damp gravel.

Whether you water orchids too much or too little shows in the roots. With too much water they rot, with too little they shrivel. In a transparent pot you see this early.

Light and temperature: the right spot

WhatRHS recommendation
Light in winterbright, an east- or west-facing windowsill is ideal
Light in summerno direct sun, it scorches the leaves
Daytime temperature19 to 30 °C
Night temperature16 to 19 °C, not below 16 °C
Avoidradiators directly below and draughts

An east- or west-facing window suits all year round. On a south-facing window you need a curtain in summer or a spot a little further from the glass.

Feeding and repotting

Watering orchids and feeding them go together. During the growing season, feed lightly but regularly with an orchid fertiliser. Every fourth time, water without feeding so that salts do not build up in the pot. In winter, the RHS says to feed sparingly, if at all.

Repotting is due about every two years, or when the plant gets too big for its pot. Here is how:

  1. Use only special bark-based orchid compost. Ordinary multipurpose compost kills the roots, according to the RHS.
  2. Cut off unhealthy roots and shorten healthy ones to about 12 centimetres.
  3. Choose a transparent pot, not too large.
  4. Firm the compost lightly, add a cane for the flower stem if needed, water and let it drain well.

After flowering: how to make your orchid flower again

Once the flowers have faded, you do not need to remove the whole stem. The RHS advises cutting it just above the second node below the spent flowers. A new flowering shoot can grow from that node. If the whole stem dies, cut it off at the base.

If a large, healthy plant has not flowered for a long time, it needs a nudge: move it for about a month to a spot that is around 5 °C cooler. According to the RHS, it should then send up a new flower stem.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I water an orchid?

About once a week during the growing season, a little less often in winter. Let the water drain away afterwards. The roots should never dry out completely and never stand in water.

How to water orchids: can I soak them?

You can. The RHS names three ways: water from above and empty the saucer, hold the pot under a gently running tap, or stand it in a bowl of water and then let it drain thoroughly.

Where do I cut the flower stem?

Just above the second node below the spent flowers. A new flowering shoot can grow there. If the stem has dried up completely, cut it off at the base.

Why has my orchid stopped flowering?

Often it lacks a temperature drop. Move the plant for about a month to a spot around 5 °C cooler. In winter it also needs plenty of light.
