Pruning olive tree: little, but at the right time

By Silvan, Beetario · updated 24 November 2026 · 4 min read

Pruning olive tree branches is a job for late spring or early summer. Remove dead, diseased and dying branches and thin the canopy a little. Because olive trees grow slowly, they need little pruning. Never prune after August, or the new growth will not harden before the frosts.

When pruning olive tree branches is due

The Royal Horticultural Society (RHS) recommends pruning in late spring or early summer. By then you can see which branches have come through the winter well and which have not.

The latest date matters just as much: not after August. Pruning stimulates new shoots. If they come too late, they cannot harden before the first frost and are damaged.

So an olive tree does not need winter pruning like an apple tree. It is evergreen and grows quite differently.

Pruning olive tree branches: 3 rules

  1. Dead and diseased out. Cut dead, diseased and dying branches back to a healthy sideshoot.
  2. Thin out. Remove individual branches so light reaches the centre of the canopy, and cut branches that spoil the shape.
  3. Keep the trunk clear. Remove shoots growing low on the trunk or from the base if you want a clear stem.

That is usually all it needs. The RHS explicitly warns against pruning any harder. Hard pruning leads to lots of vigorous, upright water shoots.

Olive trees in pots and as standards

In a pot the olive tree stays smaller. When it reaches about 1.5 metres, pinch out or trim the tips. That encourages branching.

A standard with a rounded head needs a little more attention. The RHS advises pinching or trimming the head several times from spring to late summer. Here too: not after August.

Frost damage and winter protection

Many olive trees tolerate temperatures of minus 5 to minus 10 °C, according to the RHS. Leaves and branches can still be damaged by severe frosts below minus 5 °C and by cold winds.

  • Pruning frost damage: cut out frosted shoots in late spring, back to the new growth. Before then you cannot see what has really died.
  • In the garden: in very cold winters, wrap the canopy in two layers of fleece or hessian.
  • In a pot: move it under cover or to a sheltered spot away from cold winds, and wrap pot and canopy in severe frost. In cold areas it is better to overwinter it frost-free.

Spring can still turn cold. How to judge late frost is covered in the guide to the last frost date.

Watering and care in a pot

An olive tree needs a sunny, sheltered spot and well-drained soil without waterlogging. In a pot, the RHS advises regular watering from April to late September. In winter, keep the compost barely moist and raise the pot on feet so water can drain.

A tree in the ground is fed only if it shows a deficiency. In a pot, replace the top 5 centimetres or so of compost with fresh compost every spring. The pot should be at least 30 to 35 centimetres wide and deep and have plenty of drainage holes.

Do not expect a large harvest. According to the RHS, olive trees rarely fruit in the UK and are grown mainly for their shape and foliage.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time for pruning an olive tree?

In late spring or early summer. Do not prune after August, because new shoots will not harden before the frosts.

How hard can I cut back an olive tree?

Only a little. The RHS advises removing only dead, diseased and badly placed branches and thinning the canopy a little. Hard pruning brings lots of water shoots.

How much frost can an olive tree take?

Many olive trees tolerate minus 5 to minus 10 °C, according to the RHS. Severe frost and cold winds can damage leaves and branches. Place pots in shelter or overwinter them frost-free.

Should I cut off frosted branches straight away?

No. Wait until late spring. Then cut the dead shoots back to the new growth.
