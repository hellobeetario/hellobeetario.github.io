Pruning lavender: the right time and the right height

By Silvan, Beetario · updated 24 November 2026 · 3 min read

Pruning lavender mainly means: every year in late summer, just after flowering. Cut off the spent flower stalks and about 2.5 centimetres of the leafy growth. Never cut into the old, bare wood, because lavender hardly regrows from it. In spring only a light tidy-up is allowed.

When is the time for pruning lavender?

The Royal Horticultural Society (RHS) recommends trimming lavender every year in late summer, just after flowering has finished. The plant then still has time to make new shoots before winter.

Pruning lavender in spring is needed only as an exception: if the plant looks untidy or frost has damaged shoots, you can clip the foliage lightly. The main trim stays the one after flowering.

The RHS puts lavender in a pruning group of its own, together with heather and thyme. All three grow into small sub-shrubs that become woody at the base.

Pruning lavender in 3 steps

For pruning lavender, a sharp pair of secateurs is enough. For a long lavender hedge, shears are quicker. Gather the shoots and trim them into a rounded shape so that the plant stays compact.

You do not have to throw away the cut flower stalks. Dried, they keep their scent for a long time in the wardrobe.

Why never into the old wood?

Lavender regrows poorly from bare, woody stems. The RHS stresses this explicitly. If you cut back into the old wood, bare patches often remain and do not close again.

That is why pruning lavender every year matters so much. Without it, the plant gets a little taller every year, barer at the base and falls apart in the middle. You cannot rejuvenate a very woody lavender with hard pruning. It is usually better to replace it with a young plant.

Which lavender survives the winter?

Not every lavender is equally tough. According to the RHS, English lavender (Lavandula angustifolia) and its hybrids are the hardiest. French lavender and other tender types often do not survive winter outdoors and belong in a sheltered spot or a pot.

The best time to plant is April or May. The lavender then roots in well before winter. When spring frost is still an issue is covered in the guide to the last frost date.

Pruning calendar: lavender and other garden plants

Pruning lavender is a late-summer job. Other garden plants have quite different dates. These guides show the right time and technique:

  • When to prune roses: the main pruning in late winter.
  • Pruning hydrangeas: in spring or after flowering, depending on the type.
  • When to prune apple trees: in winter, while the tree is bare.
  • Pruning an olive tree: in late spring, never after August.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time for pruning lavender?

In late summer, just after flowering. In spring you only trim lightly if the lavender looks untidy or has frost damage.

How far can I cut lavender back?

The spent flower stalks and about 2.5 centimetres of the leafy shoots. Never cut into the old, bare wood; lavender hardly regrows from it.

Can I save an old, woody lavender?

Hardly. Because lavender regrows poorly from old wood, hard pruning does not help. A young plant is usually the better solution.

Should I prune lavender in autumn?

No. The trim belongs in late summer, just after flowering. There is no separate autumn pruning.
