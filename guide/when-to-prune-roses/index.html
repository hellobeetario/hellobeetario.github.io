When to prune roses: timing and technique for every type

By Silvan, Beetario · updated 24 November 2026 · 4 min read

When to prune roses: for most types in late winter, in February or March, and in cold areas rather in March. Cut just above an outward-facing bud and remove dead, diseased, spindly and crossing wood. The exceptions are rambling roses and once-flowering shrub roses: prune them in late summer after flowering.

When to prune roses: the right month

The Royal Horticultural Society (RHS) names late winter as a good time for pruning roses, usually February or March. For hybrid teas and floribundas, start when growth is just resuming. In colder areas, wait until March.

The difference between climbers and ramblers matters. Climbing roses repeat-flower into autumn. Rambling roses usually flower only once, around June. They are therefore pruned after flowering, in late summer.

Pruning roses: the basic rules for every cut

  • Dead and diseased first. Remove dead, diseased, spindly and crossing stems. Cut dieback down to healthy white pith.
  • Air in the middle. Aim for well-spaced stems that allow free air flow.
  • Remove suckers. Trace shoots that come from the roots below the graft back to their root and pull them away.
  • Renew old wood. On established roses, cut out poorly flowering old wood and saw away old stubs that no longer produce shoots.

Whatever the answer to when to prune roses of your type, these rules stay the same. Tools must be sharp so the cuts are clean. For thicker stems use loppers or a pruning saw, and gloves protect you from thorny roses.

Pruning hybrid tea and floribunda roses

Hybrid teas and floribundas are pruned hardest. The RHS gives exact heights:

Rose typeHow hard
Hybrid tea, strong shootsto 4 to 6 buds, about 10 to 15 cm from the base
Hybrid tea, weaker shootsto 2 to 4 buds, about 5 to 10 cm from the base
Floribundato about 25 to 30 cm above soil level

Floribunda stems are left longer than hybrid tea stems. Now and then you can cut an old stem back to a few centimetres above the soil so the shrub renews itself. Newly planted roses should be pruned hard, according to the RHS, except climbing and shrub roses.

When to prune roses grown as shrubs and English roses

With shrub roses, when to prune roses depends on how often they flower:

  • Once-flowering: prune lightly in late summer after flowering. Remove dead, diseased and crossing wood and, if the centre is crowded, take out one or two old branches.
  • Repeat-flowering: in late winter, reduce strong new growth by up to a third and shorten sideshoots to two or three buds. English roses are cut back by 30 to 50 percent.

On mature repeat-flowering shrub roses, cut a few old main stems to the base every winter. In summer, deadhead faded flowers to encourage new ones.

Pruning climbing and rambling roses

Climbing roses are pruned in winter, between December and February, according to the RHS. Cut the flowered sideshoots back by two thirds. Tie in long shoots in autumn so the wind does not damage them.

Rambling roses are pruned in late summer, after flowering. Every year, remove about one in three of the oldest stems entirely. Shorten the flowered sideshoots by about two thirds. If you want to keep the hips, leave the faded flowers in place.

If a climber or rambler is completely overgrown, you can renovate it between late autumn and late winter. Keep no more than six young, vigorous stems.

After pruning: feed, mulch, deadhead

Knowing when to prune roses is half the job; aftercare is the other half. Feed pruned roses in spring. A mulch at least 5 centimetres thick, applied between late autumn and late winter, gives frost protection, according to the RHS. In summer, keep removing faded flowers so new ones follow.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time to prune roses?

For most roses, late winter, in February or March. In cold areas wait until March. Rambling roses and once-flowering shrub roses are pruned in late summer after flowering.

How low should I cut roses?

It depends on the type. Hybrid teas to 4 to 6 buds, about 10 to 15 centimetres from the base. Floribundas to 25 to 30 centimetres. Shrub roses by up to a third only.

Where exactly do I cut a rose?

No more than 5 millimetres above an outward-facing bud. The cut slopes down away from the bud so that water runs off.

I do not know my rose variety. How do I prune it?

Prune in February or March. Remove dead, diseased and crossing wood. Shorten a single thick old stem by a third to a half, according to the RHS, rather than cutting it right down.
