When to prune apple trees: winter pruning step by step

By Silvan, Beetario · updated 24 November 2026 · 4 min read

When to prune apple trees: in winter, from November to early March, while the tree is leafless. Remove diseased, weak and crossing branches, keep the centre open and shorten the previous year's growth on the main branches by a third. Take off no more than 10 to 20 percent of the canopy per winter, or the tree produces lots of water shoots.

When to prune apple trees

The Royal Horticultural Society (RHS) recommends pruning between November and early March, when the tree is leafless. For apples and pears this is the best time: without leaves you can see the shape of the canopy and spot which branches are in the way.

Winter pruning has several aims. It keeps the tree productive, removes infected growth and thins the canopy. Light then reaches the fruit so that it ripens well. At the same time more air circulates, which helps prevent fungal infections, according to the RHS.

This guide is for free-standing apple trees grown as bushes or standards. Espaliers, fans and cordons are pruned mainly in summer, according to the RHS. That is a topic of its own.

Pruning an apple tree in 4 steps

  1. Tidy up. Cut out branches that cross and rub together, weak spindly growth and anything diseased or damaged.
  2. Open the centre. According to the RHS, a free-standing tree should look like a wine glass: a clear trunk and main branches growing outwards. Larger branches in the middle and side branches growing inwards come out.
  3. Shorten the leaders. On the main branches, shorten last year's growth by about a third. Cut above a bud facing the way you want, or just above a strongly growing side branch.
  4. Leave the side shoots. Leave most side shoots unpruned, because they form the fruit buds. Thin them only where they are too crowded.

On older apple trees there are also the fruiting spurs, short knobbly shoots carrying the flower buds. If they are too congested, thin them, preferably starting on the underside of the branches.

How much can come off? The key limits

The most common mistake is too much at once. The RHS sets clear limits:

  • No more than 10 to 20 percent of the canopy per winter. Removing more than a fifth of the branches usually leads to lots of upright water shoots.
  • No haircut. Prune selected branches, not the whole canopy evenly.
  • Care with thick branches. Think twice before cutting branches over 10 to 12 centimetres in diameter. Most cuts are on branches of 1 to 5 centimetres.
  • No stubs. Cut cleanly without leaving a stump.

A well-kept apple tree often needs only 10 to 20 cuts, according to the RHS. If a tree is overgrown, spread the pruning over several winters. And if in doubt, leave a branch until next winter.

Tip bearers: varieties that are pruned differently

Most apple varieties carry their fruit on short spurs along the branches. The guide above is for them. Some varieties, however, fruit at the tips of their shoots. The RHS names, for example, 'Discovery', 'Bramley's Seedling', 'Worcester Pearmain', 'Blenheim Orange' and 'Lord Lambourne'.

On these tip bearers, do not shorten the tips. Instead, cut back some of the older branches that have already fruited to a younger shoot closer to the trunk. That stops branches breaking under the weight of fruit and keeps the canopy open.

Tools and wound care

You need sharp secateurs and a good pruning saw. Clean the tools first, for example with rubbing alcohol. Clean cuts seal over faster, according to the RHS.

Pruning paint is not needed for apples and pears. For taller apple trees, the RHS recommends a stable three-legged fruit tripod ladder.

Besides apple trees, other plants also have a fixed pruning date: roses in late winter, olive trees in late spring and lavender after flowering. In the Beetario Pro pruning module you get the dates for your garden as reminders.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time to prune apple trees?

In winter, from November to early March, when the tree is leafless. Espaliers and other trained forms are pruned mainly in summer.

How much can I cut off an apple tree?

No more than 10 to 20 percent of the canopy per winter. More than a fifth of the branches leads to lots of water shoots, according to the RHS. Prune an overgrown tree over several years.

Do I need to seal pruning cuts on an apple tree?

No. According to the RHS, apples and pears need no pruning paint. Clean cuts with sharp, clean tools are what matters.

Does this also apply to pear trees?

Yes. The RHS advice applies to apple and pear trees alike.
