Pruning hydrangeas: how to get flowers next summer

By Silvan, Beetario · updated 24 November 2026 · 4 min read

When pruning hydrangeas, the type decides. Mophead hydrangeas flower on last year's shoots: in spring you only cut back to the first strong pair of buds below the old flower head. Panicle and smooth hydrangeas can take a hard prune every spring. Climbing hydrangeas are pruned in summer after flowering.

Which hydrangea do you have?

Pruning mophead hydrangeas: less is more

The Royal Horticultural Society (RHS) gives mophead hydrangeas a pruning group of their own. They flower on shoots that grew the year before. If you cut everything back hard in spring, you cut off the summer's flowers.

Leave the faded heads on over winter. According to the RHS, they protect the buds below from frost. In early spring, cut each old flower stem back to the first strong, healthy pair of buds. In addition, remove one or two of the oldest stems at the base every year. That keeps the shrub young.

When pruning hydrangeas of this type, a complete renovation is possible, but according to the RHS it costs the flowers of the following summer.

Pruning lacecap hydrangeas

Lacecap hydrangeas are closely related to mopheads; both are Hydrangea macrophylla. For pruning hydrangeas of this kind, the RHS advises a different timing: after flowering, cut the faded heads back to the second pair of leaves below the head. Otherwise the same applies as for the mophead: no hard pruning, because it too flowers on last year's wood.

Hard pruning for panicle and smooth hydrangeas

Panicle hydrangeas and smooth hydrangeas tolerate pruning much better. Every spring, cut last year's stems back to a healthy pair of buds.

  • For large flower heads: prune hard to the lowest pair of healthy buds. According to the RHS, this creates a framework about 25 centimetres high.
  • For a taller shrub: leave a framework of about 60 centimetres.
  • Without pruning: that works too. According to the RHS, only the dead wood has to be removed in spring.

Because they take hard pruning so well, pruning hydrangeas of these two types is the easiest. You can hardly go wrong here.

Climbing hydrangeas and frost damage

The climbing hydrangea is the exception to spring pruning. The RHS advises pruning it in summer, straight after flowering, not in spring like the other types.

If a late frost has damaged young shoots, pruning hydrangeas in spring means: back to just above the first undamaged pair of buds on healthy wood. When to expect late frost is covered in the guide to the last frost date.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time for pruning hydrangeas?

For most hydrangeas, late winter or early spring. Lacecap hydrangeas are pruned after flowering, climbing hydrangeas in summer straight after flowering.

Can I prune hydrangeas in autumn?

Better not for mopheads. According to the RHS, the old flower heads protect the buds over winter. Only in early spring do you cut back to the first strong pair of buds.

Why is my mophead hydrangea not flowering?

Often it was pruned too hard. Mopheads flower on last year's shoots. Cutting them back hard in spring removes the flowers. Late frost can also destroy the buds.

How hard can I prune a panicle hydrangea?

Hard. For large flowers, down to the lowest healthy pair of buds, leaving a framework of about 25 centimetres. For a taller shrub, leave about 60 centimetres.
