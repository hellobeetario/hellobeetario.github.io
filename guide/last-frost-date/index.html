Last frost date: when tender plants can go outside

By Silvan, Beetario · updated 24 November 2026 · 5 min read

The last frost date is the average date of the last spring frost where you live. It is a starting point, not a guarantee: according to the Royal Horticultural Society, the last air frost usually comes two to four weeks before it is really safe to plant out. Tender plants go out when nights stay above 7 °C, tomatoes and peppers ideally above 10 °C.

What a last frost date tells you

A last frost date is worked out from weather records for a place: on average, after this date no more frost occurs. It tells you roughly when spring arrives in your area. But it is an average, and frost does not keep to averages.

The Royal Horticultural Society (RHS) therefore distinguishes two things. The last air frost, with temperatures near or below 0 °C, usually comes two to four weeks before the date when tender plants can safely go out. And even without a real frost, cold nights of 1 to 4 °C can damage or kill tender and half-hardy plants. They often grow slowly and poorly for weeks afterwards.

For the UK the RHS gives typical first safe planting times:

After the last frost date: 3 rules for planting out

  1. Check the night temperature. Plant tender plants out only when nights stay above 7 °C. Tomatoes, peppers, basil and cucurbits do better if nights stay above 10 °C.
  2. Read the forecast. Look at the forecast for the next 5 to 10 days, not just tomorrow.
  3. Know your site. In sheltered and urban gardens you can plant one to two weeks earlier, according to the RHS. In exposed or high sites, wait one to three weeks longer. Cold air collects in hollows, so frost pockets are planted later.

The Ice Saints: Europe's folk last frost date

In Germany, Austria and Switzerland, gardeners traditionally wait for the Ice Saints, the name days of five saints from 11 to 15 May (in Switzerland usually 12 to 15 May). The folk rule says: never plant before «Cold Sophie» on 15 May. Because of the calendar reform of 1582, the dates would fall around 20 to 23 May in the old calendar, as SRF Meteo explains.

Weather services have tested the rule. wetterdienst.de found that over the last 50 years the Ice Saints brought a minimum of 3 °C or less in only about a third of years. MeteoSwiss found no more ground frost during the Ice Saints than in the days before or after, over about 60 years of records. Ground frost in May is not rare, but it becomes much less frequent by the end of the month. A fixed date is therefore no substitute for the forecast.

Late frost forecast: how to protect your plants

  • Use fleece. According to the RHS, heavier grades of horticultural fleece give about 2 °C of protection. On the coldest nights, use a double layer. Fleece is so light that it needs no hoops.
  • Cover at night. Protect plants at night when low temperatures are forecast.
  • Bring pots in. Move larger container plants under cover on cool nights until temperatures are stable. For a sudden sharp frost, the RHS suggests an old curtain over the fleece.
  • After damage. Frost can scorch young growth. The RHS advises cutting out the damaged parts and continuing to harden the plant off.

Frost damage on shrubs is a topic of its own. How to cut back frosted shoots on hydrangeas or an olive tree is covered in the pruning guides.

Frequently asked questions

What is a last frost date?

The average date of the last spring frost at a place, worked out from weather records. Frost can still occur after it, so use it as a guide and check the forecast.

When is it safe to plant tomatoes outside?

The Royal Horticultural Society says when nights stay above 7 °C; tomatoes, peppers and basil do better with nights above 10 °C. In much of England that is the second half of May, further north later.

How much does fleece protect against frost?

Heavier grades of fleece give about 2 °C of protection, according to the RHS. On very cold nights, use a double layer.

Are the Ice Saints a reliable last frost date?

No. MeteoSwiss found no more ground frost during the Ice Saints than at other times in May. Ground frost becomes much rarer only towards the end of May.
