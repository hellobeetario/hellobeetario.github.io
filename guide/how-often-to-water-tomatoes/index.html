How often to water tomatoes, and how much

By Silvan, Beetario · updated 24 November 2026 · 5 min read

How often to water tomatoes depends on the weather and the pot, but one thing always counts: evenness. The soil should stay slightly moist, never dry out completely and never stay waterlogged. Water less often but thoroughly, preferably in the morning and at the roots. Tomatoes in pots often need water every day in hot weather.

Why tomatoes need even watering

The Royal Horticultural Society (RHS) advises watering tomatoes regularly so that the soil stays evenly moist. This matters most during flowering and while the fruit is swelling.

If moisture swings a lot, you see it in the fruit:

  • Split fruit: after a dry spell the plant suddenly takes up a lot of water, and the skin splits.
  • Blossom end rot: a dark, sunken patch appears at the bottom of the fruit. According to the RHS, irregular watering is the cause. Peppers and aubergines are affected too.

Neither can be undone once it appears. The only prevention is regular watering.

How often to water tomatoes in beds and pots

There is no fixed answer to how often to water tomatoes. It depends on the weather, the soil and the container. The best test is your finger:

WhereHow oftenWhat to watch
Bedless often, but thoroughlydown into the root zone, about 15 cm deep
Raised bedmore often than in a beddries out faster
Pots and containersoften daily in hot weatherwater when the compost is dry 2 to 3 cm down

In beds, the RHS advises watering less often but more thoroughly and letting the surface dry in between. NABU explains why: roots then grow deeper and the plant copes better with dry days. Watering a little every day keeps the roots near the surface.

In a raised bed and in pots it is different, because a small volume of soil dries out quickly. The RHS advises checking containers daily from spring to autumn.

When and how to water tomatoes

  • In the morning. According to the RHS, early morning is the best time. Less water evaporates and the plant has it for the day. NABU also recommends the morning for vegetables.
  • Not over the leaves. Leaves that stay wet overnight encourage fungal diseases, according to the RHS. Water straight onto the soil at the base.
  • Thoroughly. The water should reach the roots. In a pot, fill to the rim, let it soak in and water again.
  • Empty the saucer. Roots should not stand in water. Empty the saucer after a few hours.
  • Use rainwater. The RHS advises collecting rainwater and using tap water only when the butt is empty.

A mulch of grass clippings or straw keeps moisture in the soil. The RHS recommends 5 to 7.5 centimetres, applied after rain or watering.

Watering and feeding tomatoes

Watering tomatoes and feeding them go together. Once the first fruits start to swell, tomatoes in pots need extra nutrients. The RHS recommends a high-potassium liquid feed every 10 to 14 days, added to the watering can. In well-prepared garden soil this is often not needed, according to the RHS, but it can increase the yield.

Cordon tomatoes also need their side shoots removed. Pinch out the shoots that grow where the leaves meet the main stem, ideally every time you water. Once the plant reaches the top of its cane, stop the main stem two leaves above the top truss.

Without a rain cover, choose varieties tolerant of late blight. The Bavarian State Institute for Viticulture and Horticulture advises this for tomatoes in raised beds.

Frequently asked questions

How much water do tomatoes need?

There is no fixed amount. What matters is that the water reaches the root zone, about 15 centimetres deep in a bed. In a pot, water until it runs out at the bottom.

Should I water tomatoes in the morning or evening?

Preferably in the morning. Evening works too, but leaves that stay wet overnight encourage fungal diseases, according to the RHS. Always water straight onto the soil.

Why are my tomatoes splitting?

Usually because of irregular watering. After a dry spell the plant suddenly takes up a lot of water and the skin splits. Evenly moist soil and a mulch help prevent it.

How often to water tomatoes in pots?

Often, in hot weather. Check with your finger: if the compost is dry 2 to 3 centimetres down, water.
